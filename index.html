PROBLEM STATEMENT

THE LAST ARCHIVE

Problem Statement Year 2179. Humanity is no longer the dominant species on Earth. Following a devastating extraterrestrial invasion, human civilization has collapsed. Cities have fallen, governments have disappeared, and most records of human civilization have been lost. A small group of survivors has managed to establish a secure digital archive containing fragments of information recovered from the old world. But there is a problem.

Future generations know almost nothing about the humans who came before them. Your task is to build a digital portal into the life of an ordinary human from the 21st century a futuristic archive that allows someone from the future to understand what humanity was really like. The website should allow users to explore different aspects of human civilization, such as:

Humans : What did humans look like? How did they live?

Food : What did humans eat? How was food produced and prepared?

Economy : How did money, jobs, businesses and trade work?

Daily Life : Where did people live? What did an ordinary day look like?

Geography : What did Earth and its cities look like?

Culture : What music, art, entertainment and traditions existed?

Society : How were communities, governments and institutions organized?

Technology : What technologies did humans create and depend on?

Relationships : How did humans communicate, socialize and form relationships?

History : What major events shaped human civilization?

These are only starting points. You decide what parts of humanity deserve to be remembered.

What We Are Looking For


Participants must design and develop a functional, responsive and immersive web experience that presents human civilization as if it were being discovered by a future society. The website should feel like a futuristic digital archive, encyclopedia, museum, database, or portal into the lost human world. We encourage participants to experiment with: * Futuristic interfaces and visual systems
* Interactive timelines and maps
* Searchable archives and categories
* Data visualizations
* Interactive stories
* Images, videos and historical artifacts
* Animations and micro-interactions
* Creative navigation systems
* Any other web-based interaction that strengthens the experience
The solution does not need to cover every aspect of human civilization. Instead, participants should choose a clear direction and execute it exceptionally well.

Evaluation Criteria


1. Creativity & Originality: 25% How unique and imaginative is the concept?
2. UI/UX & Visual Design: 20% How immersive, intuitive and visually compelling is the experience?
3. Functionality: 20% How well does the website actually work?
4. Technical Implementation: 20% How effectively are web technologies used to bring the concept to life?
5. Storytelling & Presentation: 15% How effectively does the website communicate the story of lost humanity?
